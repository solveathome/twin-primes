Every integer gets a column, and every stacked prime gets a row. A prime's block starts at its own row and hangs down until it meets the next prime that struck. So the skyline is the largest stacked prime dividing each number, and empty is a hole. Two holes two apart is a slot. the grain census → the dimension of a hole →

A slot's bar is filled when it sits below the frontier p², where holes are certified primes and the slot is a real twin prime pair. It is hollow past the frontier, where a later prime can still kill it.

The tile is a palindrome. Fold the picture along the mirror axis and every strike column lands on another strike column, so the axis is drawn at the centre of each tile, at p#/2. Reflection about 0 is the same map mod p#, one symmetry whose two fixed points sit half a period apart, so the boundary axis is forced by periodicity and shows nothing the centre does not. The internal seams of the last fold are axes of the previous tile, not of this one: the new prime's strikes break all but this one.
Boundaries and positions are drawn differently. A tile edge is a boundary between copies, so it sits at the left edge of its column: a fold lays down whole sets of positions, never half a position. A mirror axis is a position, so it runs through the middle of its column, because folding there has to land that column on itself. The frontier is a boundary too: everything strictly below p² is settled, and p² itself is composite.

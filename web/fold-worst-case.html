the fold ladder, worst case — adversarial vs pinned, natal@5 onward

Fold the twin-slot comb by one prime at a time. Each fold in the covering form strikes two classes {ap, ap−2} mod p: channel A takes the lower member of the pair, channel B the upper. The adversary picks ap; real folding is pinned at ap = 0. Everything below is computed inside the window [0, N) only — a segment, never a tile.

honesty, kept visible

  • (i) Greedy worst case per fold is NOT the joint covering optimum. This tool picks the worst single alignment given the survivors it happens to have. The true adversary optimises all folds jointly — branch-and-bound territory — and can accept a weak fold now to buy a worse one later. Watch for the give-away: the greedy track sometimes scores below the pinned track at a later fold, because its own earlier choices left it a different survivor set.
  • (ii) The window is [0, N), a segment, not the tile. Worst-case-in-window is a lower bound on worst-case-in-tile. And the converse bites: on a whole period all p alignments are conjugate (translate by a multiple of the previous tile width), so the adversary gains exactly nothing — the misalignment principle, THE-LENS.md §3. When the readout below says the window covers one or more full periods, expect the ranking to be flat or nearly so. That flatness is the mathematics, not a bug.
  • (iii) Units are on every number: slots or integers. A twin slot occurs once per m̄ integers, so the two counts differ by that factor and are never comparable raw (qc/units.js item 2, the corpus's most expensive confusion).
what am i looking at

Twin slot. A position r where both r and r+2 are still holes — no folded prime has hit either one. It is a possible twin pair, not a promised one. Below the frontier p′² every surviving slot is a real twin prime; above it, a candidate.

Natal@5. The comb you start from: fold 3 and 5 and what is left is r ≡ 11, 17, 29 (mod 30). Those are the three houses, and every twin pair above 5 lives in one of them forever. The ribbon's leftmost ticks are 11, 17, 29, 41, 47, 59, …

Fold. Adding prime p lays p copies of the tile end to end and strikes p residue classes through every copy. On twin slots that is exactly two classes deleted from one fixed pattern. A composite fold kills nothing (Inertness Lemma), so only primes appear on the ladder.

Channel A and channel B. The fold kills class ap and class ap−2. A slot dies to A when p hits its lower member r, to B when p hits its upper member r+2. The two channels are the same set translated by 2, and how they interleave is the whole structure — so they get separate colours everywhere.

Worst vs pinned. Pinned is ap = 0, which is what real sieving does: p strikes its own multiples. Worst is the adversary in the covering form, free to translate the pair to wherever it hurts most. Same symbols, opposite freedom (qc/units.js item 5).

Dead run, and L. A stretch of consecutive twin slots all killed by the same fold. Its length in slots is L, the adjacent-kill run; the hole it leaves, measured in integers, is the new gap. Long runs are what make G₂ grow, and CRT scattering is what keeps them short.
